# The Creator Squad

The UGC pillar is the only one that scales without your team's time and without spending trust you haven't earned. It's also the slowest and the easiest to run badly. This is the version that works at a normal brand's scale.

---

## Scale honestly

The source video describes 100,000 invites, 6,000 creators onboarded and ~5,800 videos in 60 days for an agency client. That's an agency-operated programme with dedicated automation tooling behind it, and the operational load is a full-time job.

**Start with 25–50 creators.** At 2–3 videos each that's 50–150 assets a quarter, which is more than enough to keep the UGC row of a 15-cell board full with plenty spare. Scale the squad only when creator supply — not editing, not budget, not angles — is the thing capping your launches.

---

## The pipeline

```
Source → Invite → Approve → Ship sample → Brief → Deliver → Ingest → Feed back
```

**Source.** Short-form platforms first, in your category and adjacent ones. Look for people who already make the kind of video you want, at any follower count — a 900-follower creator who films well is worth more than a 90k account that films badly, because you're buying assets, not reach. Also invite your own customers: people who already left a five-star review are the highest-converting creators you'll ever get, and they need no convincing about the product.

**Invite.** Keep it short: what the product is, what you're offering, what you want back, deadline. Whatever tool sends it — a platform's collab tool, an automation service, or a spreadsheet and a mail merge — the response rate lives in the offer, not the automation.

**Approve.** Check they post real video, in your language/market, with a face and a voice. Skip anyone whose feed is entirely reposts.

**Ship.** Bundle enough product for the usage period the brief asks for. Under-shipping is the most common cause of thin, unconvincing content — a creator who used it twice makes a video that looks like they used it twice.

**Brief.** Two types, both in `creative-briefs.md`: **wide** for discovery, **specific** for scaling a proven angle. Run roughly 30% wide / 70% specific once you have winners to point at.

**Deliver.** Ask for **2–3 videos per sample**, stated up front and built into the offer. This is the difference between a creator programme that's cheaper than AI and one that isn't. Set a firm date and chase once.

**Ingest.** File every asset by cell (pillar × stage) the moment it lands, not when you need it. An un-filed library is an unused library.

**Feed back.** Tell the squad what's winning, with examples. This is the compounding step and almost nobody does it.

---

## Timeline reality

Ship (up to a week) → the creator's own backlog (a week or more) → usage period → filming and posting. **Expect 1–4 weeks from invite to usable asset.**

Consequence: **the creator pillar cannot be your emergency supply.** When a cell empties this week, AI fills it. Creators are the standing order you place a month ahead. Plan the squad's output on a rolling 4-week horizon or you'll always be short.

---

## The community layer

Once you're past ~20 creators, put them somewhere together — a group chat, a Discord, an email list, whatever's free. What it's for:

- **Publishing what's winning.** "This ad did 7× on $10k spend, here's why, make more like it." Turns a random-output programme into a targeted one.
- **Re-activation.** A new product, offer or seasonal push goes to people who already have your product and already know how to film it. Thousands of assets in weeks is only realistic *because the squad already exists* — the second campaign is the one that pays for the first.
- **Incentives.** Early access, commission, product credit, a monthly best-video prize. Cheaper than raising rates and it selects for the people who care.
- **Standards.** Disclosure requirements, the restricted-claims list, and format specs in one place everyone can see.

## Cost tracking

Per usable asset:
```
cost/asset = (COGS + shipping + incentives + your ops time) / usable videos received
```
"Usable" means it made it into an ad set. Track it monthly against your AI cost per usable asset and your in-house cost per usable asset. That comparison is what the 33/33/33 target should eventually be re-tuned against — the split is a sensible starting allocation, not a law.

Expect the creator pillar to look expensive in month one and cheap by month three, as the squad becomes re-usable and the videos-per-sample ratio climbs.

---

## Non-negotiables

- **Disclosure on every collaboration.** Gifted product is compensation. Paid-partnership label plus a visible disclosure — ACCC in Australia, FTC in the US, and platform policy on top.
- **Written permission for paid usage.** Organic permission is not ads permission. Get whitelisting/usage rights in the invite terms, with a term length, or you'll build a library you can't run.
- **A restricted-claims list in every brief.** Creators will say things you can't legally say. That's your liability, not theirs.
- **Don't script authenticity out.** The value of this pillar is that it doesn't sound like you. Over-brief it and you've paid a stranger to make an in-house ad.
