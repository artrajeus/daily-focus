# Angles, Scripts and Briefs

How to turn an empty cell into something a person can actually shoot, design or generate.

---

## Finding angles (before you write anything)

Angles come from evidence, not brainstorms. In priority order:

1. **Your own winners.** Pull the top-spending ads and read what they actually say — the transcript, the on-screen text, the first three seconds. The claim that's working is rarely the one in the brief. Extract it, then rebuild it in the other two pillars.
2. **Reviews and support inbox.** The exact sentences customers use. Search reviews for "I was worried", "I didn't expect", "instead of", "finally". Those four phrases are an angle each: objection, surprise, comparison, relief.
3. **Creator outtakes.** The line a creator improvised that wasn't in your brief. This is the single most common source of a step-change angle, because creators aren't carrying your assumptions about the product.
4. **Competitor creative.** What the category is running, so you can find the axis nobody's on.
5. **The product itself.** Ingredients, materials, manufacturing decision, what you left out. The "what's NOT in it" angle is under-used in almost every category.

**Convert each finding into one line:** `For [avatar], who [problem], [product] is the [category] that [differentiator], unlike [villain].` If you can't fill that line, you don't have an angle yet.

## Script skeletons by stage

Written for 15–30s video. Cut the same structure to a static by keeping the hook and the payoff.

### Unaware — stat hook
```
HOOK (0-3s)   [Number] of [group] have [problem] and don't know it.
TURN (3-8s)   Here's what it actually looks like: [3 everyday symptoms they'd recognise]
BRIDGE (8-15) It's usually [cause]. Which is fixable.
SOFT CTA      [Product] was built for exactly this. Link below.
```

### Unaware — founder story
```
HOOK      I quit [thing] because I couldn't find [product] that [requirement].
STAKES    Here's what went wrong when I tried: [specific, unflattering, true]
BUILD     So we made [product]. [One decision you made differently and why]
CTA       If you've had the same problem, it's linked below.
```
The unflattering detail is what makes it work. A founder story with no setback is an advert; one with a setback is a story.

### Problem-aware — before/after
```
HOOK      [State A], on day one.
PROOF     Same person, day [N]. [Visible change, honestly framed]
MECHANISM What changed: [product] + [what they actually did]
CTA       [Offer]
```

### Problem-aware — three reasons why
```
HOOK      Three reasons I switched to [category], not [villain].
1         [Benefit] — because [mechanism]
2         [Benefit] — because [mechanism]
3         [Benefit] — because [mechanism]
CTA       [Product], linked.
```

### Solution-aware — us vs them
```
HOOK      [Category] vs [villain] — the difference nobody mentions.
SPLIT     [Axis 1]: them [X], us [Y]
          [Axis 2]: them [X], us [Y]
          [Axis 3]: them [X], us [Y]
HONESTY   They're better at [one thing]. If that's your priority, buy theirs.
CTA       Everyone else: [product].
```
The honesty line raises conversion and keeps you out of trouble on comparative-advertising rules.

### Solution-aware — ingredient/spec breakdown
```
HOOK      Read the back of [villain]. I'll wait.
READ      [List the long/vague list, unhurried]
CONTRAST  Ours: [short list], and here's what each one does.
CTA       That's the whole reason I switched.
```

### Product-aware — objection handling
```
HOOK      I nearly didn't buy this because [real objection].
ADDRESS   Here's what actually happened: [answer]
PROOF     [Guarantee / result / timeframe]
CTA       [Offer]
```

### Most-aware — direct
```
HOOK      [Product]. [Price/offer]. [Deadline if real].
BODY      [One benefit, one proof point]
CTA       Buy — link below.
```
Under 12 seconds. If it's longer, it's a Product-aware ad wearing the wrong hat.

## Hook patterns that carry across stages

- Number opener — "74% of…"
- Contradiction — "Everything you've been told about X…"
- Confession — "I nearly didn't…"
- Direct address — "If you [specific situation], this is for you."
- Demonstration — start mid-action, no preamble.
- Comparison — two things on screen in the first frame.

**Hook rate is the test, not opinion.** Same body, four hooks, is a legitimate use of a variant — but it lives inside one cell, it is not four cells.

## Creator brief templates

### Wide brief — for discovery
Send when you want angles you wouldn't have written.

```
PRODUCT     [name, what it does, how to use it]
DO          Use it for [N] days first. Film honestly.
            Tell us what you actually noticed — including anything that surprised you.
DON'T       Don't read a script. Don't claim [restricted claims list].
FORMAT      Vertical, 15-40s, good light, your own voice. 2-3 different videos.
DISCLOSURE  Tag as a paid partnership / include #ad — required.
DEADLINE    [date]
```

### Specific brief — for scaling a proven angle
Send when you already know what works. This is the brief that compounds.

```
WHAT'S WORKING   Here are 3 of our top ads right now: [links/refs]
                 They work because: [the actual mechanism, one line each]
YOUR VERSION     Make [N] videos in this style, in your own words and setting.
MUST INCLUDE     [the claim/proof/beat that makes the format work]
MUST NOT         [restricted claims, competitor names, medical language]
FORMAT           Vertical, 15-40s, hook in the first 2 seconds.
DISCLOSURE       Paid partnership label required.
DEADLINE         [date]
```

Showing creators what's winning is the single highest-return thing you can do with a creator programme. Most brands never do it — they brief blind, get random output, and conclude UGC doesn't work.

## AI production briefs

Give the generator the same brief you'd give a designer, plus placement specs.

```
CELL        [pillar × stage]
ANGLE       [the one-line angle]
FORMAT      [static / video / carousel]
COPY        Headline: [ ]  Body: [ ]  On-screen text: [ ]
VISUAL      [subject, setting, mood, product placement, brand colours]
PLACEMENTS  1:1, 4:5, 9:16
CONSTRAINTS No synthetic people presenting as customers. No fabricated results.
            No claim not on the approved list.
```

**Use AI for:** statics, backgrounds, product-in-context, b-roll, offer/price layouts, voiceover, format conversion, and cheap variants of a claim that has already proven itself.

**Don't use AI for:** testimonials, before/afters of people, "real customer" framing, or anything that depends on the viewer believing a specific person had a specific experience. That's the trust the creator pillar exists to buy, and faking it costs more than it saves.

Batch generate, then **review every asset against the brief before it reaches an ad account**. AI drift — a generator wandering off the angle into something generic and pretty — is the main reason the AI pillar underperforms.

## Personification (avatar expansion)

Same product, different person, different problem.

**Trigger — all of these together:**
- Spend flat or capped
- Frequency climbing
- CPM climbing
- CPA drifting up
- Creative refills no longer moving the numbers

That combination means you've saturated an audience, not that your creative got worse. More angles for the same avatar won't fix it.

**How to build the next avatar**

1. **List the problems your product actually solves** — not the one you lead with. All of them.
2. **Give each problem a person.** For a sleep product: can't fall asleep / wakes groggy / wakes through the night. Three products' worth of messaging, one SKU.
3. **Add situational avatars** — the athlete, the shift worker, the new parent, the frequent traveller, the gift buyer. Situation changes the Unaware rung completely.
4. **Validate cheaply before committing.** Two or three AI statics on the new angle. If hook rate and CTR clear account average, build the avatar a full board.
5. **Give the winner its own 15 cells.** A new avatar with three ads bolted onto an old board will be starved by the existing winners.

**Sequence matters:** one avatar, full board, scale to plateau, then expand. Opening three avatars on day one splits a small budget into three underfunded accounts and teaches you nothing.
